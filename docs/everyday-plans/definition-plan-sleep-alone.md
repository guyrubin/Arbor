| # | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pattern id, title** | `plan-sleep-alone`. EN "My bed, my sleep". HE "המיטה שלי, השינה שלי". Names the win (my own bed, my own sleep), not the problem. |
| 2 | **Shelf and age band** | Sleep shelf. Age bands `24m`–`6-8y`; register band 5–7 (the 3–4 cut comes with the band pass). |
| 3 | **Parent words** | EN "Falls asleep only with me in the room". HE "נרדם או נרדמת רק כשאני בחדר". |
| 4 | **Body fact** | "When the room gets dark and quiet, my body gets sleepy. My eyes feel heavy, my breathing goes slow, and my body knows how to sleep all by itself." Picture: the bedroom at night, the bed (PS1). |
| 5 | **The hard part, named** | "Sometimes, when it is time to sleep, I want my grown-up to stay right next to me. The room feels big, and my eyes want to stay open. So I call, and I call again." Pose `hug-knees`; nobody scolds. |
| 6 | **The plan, three steps** | Default: (1) the same four steps every night before bed — `four-steps`; (2) my grown-up sits by the bed and every few nights the chair moves a little further, the family's pace — `sit-further`; (3) the morning check-in — `morning-check-in`. Alternative slot 2: `check-back` (goodnight, step to the door, brief calm check-ins). No step leaves a distressed child without a response; no target for the child; no named method. |
| 7 | **The box** | My cuddly toy, a small night light, one story, a quiet song. |
| 8 | **The win** | "Night after night, my body learns to fall asleep in my own bed. We do the same four steps, my grown-up is near, and my body knows how to sleep. That is my plan!" Frame line: "{name}'s body knows how to sleep." |
| 9 | **Sources** | `aap-bedtime-routine` (AAP HealthyChildren bedtime trouble / healthy sleep habits) · `nhs-sleep-young-children` (NHS sleep tips for young children) · `mindell-williamson-2018` (Sleep Medicine Reviews, bedtime routine) · `meltzer-mindell-2014` (J Pediatr Psychol review: gradual parental withdrawal, brief check-ins, family-set pace). Same evidence family as Steady Nights. |
| 10 | **Red lines (watchFor)** | loud snoring most nights · pauses in breathing during sleep · falls asleep during the day far more than children their age · under one year old · the fear started suddenly after a frightening event. Any one routes to the doctor and offers no plan. |
| 11 | **Banned words for this pattern** | EN insomnia, sleep training, cry it out, self-soothe, regression. HE נדודי שינה, אימון שינה, לתת לבכות, רגרסיה. Plus the shared §5 lists. |
| 12 | **Pose list** | Six of the shared twelve: `hug-knees` (cover, p3), `stand-tall` (p1), `lie-down` (p2, step 2), `point-up` (p4), `sit-calm` (steps 1 and 3), `hands-up` (win). |
| 13 | **The sceptic's three** | (a) gradual withdrawal is a sleep-training method and some children get more distressed → the family sets the pace, every call is answered, no method is named; (b) night fear can be sleep-disordered breathing or a frightening event → red lines; (c) a story cannot replace the routine → the routine and the chair are the parent's week in Today. |
