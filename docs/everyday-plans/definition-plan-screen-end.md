# Definition — `plan-screen-end`

Plan-book standard §1, the thirteen fields. Manuscript:
`app/src/lib/library/plans/patterns/planScreenEnd.ts`. Reviewer's yes: **not
recorded** (B-GA-37 pending, EN and HE). Hebrew: native review owed.

| # | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pattern id, title** | `plan-screen-end`. EN "Two more, then we go". HE "עוד שניים, ואז הולכים". Names the plan's own words, not the fight. |
| 2 | **Shelf and age band** | Family shelf. Age bands `24m`–`6-8y`; register band 5–7. |
| 3 | **Parent words** | EN "Ending screens or switching activities ends in a fight". HE "סיום מסכים או מעבר בין פעילויות נגמר בריב". |
| 4 | **Body fact** | "When I am deep inside a show or a game, my eyes and my ears are busy. My body forgets the room around me. It needs a little time to come back, like a diver coming up for air." Picture: the sofa and the glowing tablet (PE2). |
| 5 | **The hard part, named** | "Sometimes the screen goes off all at once, in the middle of the best part. My body is not ready. My voice gets loud, my feet stamp, and the next thing feels like a wall." Pose `hug-knees`. |
| 6 | **The plan, three steps** | Default: (1) the warning in my units: two more songs, one more round, the end of this episode — `warning-in-my-units`; (2) a timer I can see — `timer-i-can-see`; (3) the next thing has a name and a picture, and we go there together — `next-thing-pictured`. Alternative slot 2: `my-job-at-the-end` (the child presses the button or closes the lid). |
| 7 | **The box** | A sand timer, picture cards of the next things, I press the button, our "two more" song. |
| 8 | **The win** | "Day by day, my body learns that the end is coming and what comes after. Two more, then we go. My body gets ready for the next thing, and the next thing is good too. That is my plan!" Frame line: "{name}'s body is ready for the next thing." |
| 9 | **Sources** | `aap-media-young-minds` (AAP, Media and Young Minds 2016, and the Family Media Plan) · `transition-warning-evidence` (the public evidence on transition warnings, visual timers and visual schedules for young children; no brand). |
| 10 | **Red lines (watchFor)** | hurts themself when a screen ends · every change all day ends in a long storm the child cannot come down from · started suddenly after a frightening event · screens are replacing sleep or meals most days. |
| 11 | **Banned words for this pattern** | EN addiction, addicted, screen addiction, meltdown, tantrum, ADHD. HE התמכרות, מכור/ה, התקף זעם. Plus the shared §5 lists. |
| 12 | **Pose list** | Five: `hands-up` (cover, win), `stand-tall` (p1, alt step 2), `sit-calm` (p2, steps 1 and 2), `hug-knees` (p3), `point-up` (p4, step 3). |
| 13 | **The sceptic's three** | (a) warnings and timers teach negotiation → the words are said once, the timer ends the screen, the end comes exactly then; (b) all-day storms at every transition may be more than a parenting pattern → red lines, no condition named; (c) the "next thing" becomes a bribe → the next thing is the ordinary next thing, named and pictured, never a treat for compliance. |
