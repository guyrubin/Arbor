# Arbor — Partner overview

*The AI parenting companion · Private beta · For families with children to age 12*

## More understanding for you. More possibility for them.

Arbor brings together child-development knowledge, a growing picture of your child, and personal stories and play where they can be the hero.

Find a useful next step. Keep the context that matters. Make more of your time together.

## One Arbor. Three connected experiences.

### Parenting Companion — A companion for the everyday.

Talk through a difficult moment, find words that fit, and choose a manageable next step. Return with what happened, so the conversation has continuity.

### Growth — A fuller picture, over time.

Keep observations, milestones, routines and questions together. Notice change and prepare the context you want to share with your own professionals.

### Kids Studio — Their imagination, made personal.

Original stories, illustrated books and playful choices, with a familiar personal hero. The parent shapes the experience; the child has a world to explore and favorites to revisit.

## Continuity is the difference.

A generic answer ends with the conversation. Arbor is designed to connect what you ask, what you choose to try, what you notice afterwards, and the next useful step. Stories and shared play can also be valuable on their own.

You choose what is remembered, shared and opened for your child.

## A practical starting point for partners.

We are developing Arbor in Hebrew for families in Israel first. We begin with a focused experience, a defined partner role and evidence of recurring family value.

### Practitioners and parenting organizations

Explore a small, voluntary referral group. Parents use Arbor at home and choose what to bring back to their own professional. Agree the offer, support responsibility, attribution and review date before starting.

### Professional and academic reviewers

Review a defined age, language and content scope: source fidelity, practical wording, escalation boundaries or shared-story quality. Identify the reviewer, deliverable, time and fee; keep professional review distinct from endorsement or outcome research.

### Creators and content partners

Explore one original story-and-play sample, a clear editorial brief and explicit rights. Future audience work should be directed to parents and assessed against useful family participation.

## One company. A connected business.

Parenting Companion and Kids Studio form one Arbor family subscription experience, supported by Growth. Kids Studio is also the shared creative production capability behind Arbor’s planned digital channels and products.

### What comes next

Public age-banded children’s channels, a parent-facing channel and a printed personal book are planned workstreams. They are subject to product readiness, editorial quality, identity consistency, rights and unit economics. They are not presented as services already available.

## What a first collaboration should tell us

Do families reach a useful first experience? Do they return with context or revisit a story? Will they pay for recurring value? What does serving them cost? Report eligible families and observation windows alongside the result.

## Start with a 20-minute fit conversation.

Choose one family need, one partner role and one concrete deliverable. The next step is a scoped discussion, not a broad partnership announcement.
